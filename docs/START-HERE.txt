A9 Camera for Mac — 0.4.0 experimental backend preview

Apple Silicon, macOS 26+. Ad hoc signed, not Apple notarized.

DIRECT VIDEO
Join the camera hotspot in macOS. In Camera Wi-Fi, choose I joined camera
Wi-Fi — connect. Usual IP: 192.168.169.1, TCP 6123. Automatic protocol
selection tries classic then newer only for eligible stream/protocol failures.

WI-FI SETUP
Use Wi-Fi setup to connect a fresh Bluetooth session or join the classic
camera hotspot. Enter the 2.4 GHz SSID/password and check the recipient before
sending. A disconnect without acknowledgement is unconfirmed. Check the
router before resending. Passwords are not saved or logged.

HOME VIDEO
Station-mode cameras may connect outward instead of listening for direct-IP
video. Home video provides a native local gateway for that path.
- Select the Mac IP, camera IP and UID from the camera label/hotspot.
- Configure the camera's DNS so v720.naxclow.com and v720.p2p.naxclow.com
  resolve to this Mac. This is a router/camera DNS step; setting only the Mac's
  resolver does not configure the camera.
- Start the gateway. Copy its relay command, review and run it in Terminal.
  macOS requires administrator permission to bind the camera's legacy ports.
  The relay drops to your user before handling traffic and installs no service.
  Stop the relay with Control-C. Never send the admin password in a chat.
- Restart the camera and watch bootstrap, registration, media and video status.
The optional camera DNS responder is not a general household DNS resolver.

SECURITY AND LIMITS
The main app is sandboxed and hardened. The explicitly started gateway accepts
only the configured camera IP/UID/session token. The temporary relay is a
separately hardened command-line program; after binding it runs as your user
outside the app sandbox. It performs no filesystem or shell operations.
The firmware protocols are plaintext and do not cryptographically verify the
camera. Prefer a dedicated trusted camera network. Firmware can independently
contact vendor services.

Automated tests use fictitious cameras and high localhost ports. They do not
prove hardware compatibility or production privileged-port startup. See the
attached test/security reports for this release's completed checks.
MJPEG/snapshots are supported. H.264, audio, recording and SD management are not.
